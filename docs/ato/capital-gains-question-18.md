# Question 18 Capital gains — the net-capital-gain method (worked examples)

> **Source:** https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/individual-supplementary-tax-return-2026-instructions/income-questions-13-24-supplementary-tax-return-2026/18-capital-gains-2026
> ("18 Capital gains 2026", QC 106842, last updated 30 May 2026)
> **Retrieved:** 2026-07-29 from the Australian Taxation Office (ato.gov.au)
> This is a local copy of ATO guidance for reference (excerpt: the step order and
> the Kathleen worked examples). The ATO site is authoritative.

---

This is the individual tax return's own statement of the order in which a net
capital gain is worked out. The label mapping for the same question lives in
[`tax-return-labels-2026.md`](tax-return-labels-2026.md); this file carries the
**method** and its worked examples.

## The step order

- **Step 6 — Applying current year capital losses.** Deduct current-year capital
  losses from current-year capital gains.

  > When applying your current year capital losses, you can choose the method
  > that gives you the best result to reduce your current year capital gains.
  > While you will need to consider your own situation, for most people the
  > order that usually gives the greatest benefit and the smallest net capital
  > gain is to apply the capital losses against capital gains calculated using
  > the:
  >
  > - 'other' method
  > - indexation method
  > - discount method.

- **Step 7 — Applying net capital losses from earlier years.** Deduct any
  unapplied net capital losses carried forward from earlier income years.
- **Step 8 — Applying the CGT discount.**

  > You can now reduce any remaining current year capital gains calculated using
  > the discount method by the discount percentage (50% for individuals plus any
  > additional affordable housing capital gain discount for eligible investors).
  > You can't apply the discount to capital gains calculated using the indexation
  > method or the 'other' method.

- **Step 10 — Working out your net capital gain**, written at label **A**.
- **Step 11 — Capital losses carried forward** to later income years, at label **V**.

Losses therefore come off **before** the discount, and current-year losses are
applied before earlier-year losses.

## Example 1: sale of shares and collectables (Kathleen)

> Kathleen sold some assets during the year and has the following capital gains
> and capital losses for 2025–26:
>
> **Capital gain on the sale of 1,000 shares for $6 each on 17 December 2025**
>
> Kathleen bought these shares on 17 November 2000 and each has a cost base of
> $3 (including incidental costs of acquisition and disposal).
> Capital gain = $6,000 − $3,000 = $3,000
> Kathleen calculates her capital gain using the discount method.
>
> **Capital gain on the sale of 130 shares for $8 each on 27 February 2026**
>
> Kathleen bought these shares on 10 October 2025 and each has a cost base of $4
> (including incidental costs of acquisition and disposal). As the asset was
> bought and sold within 12 months, Kathleen must use the 'other' method to
> calculate her capital gain from these shares:
> (130 × $8) − (130 × $4) = $520
>
> **Capital loss on the sale of jewellery for $1,000 on 1 April 2026**
>
> Kathleen bought this jewellery for $1,500 and sold it 6 months later for
> $1,000.
> She calculates her capital loss as follows:
> $1,000 − $1,500 = $500 capital loss
>
> … First, Kathleen writes her total current year capital gains of $3,520
> ($3,000 + $520) from her shares at label H Total current year capital gains.
> This is the amount before deducting any capital losses or applying the CGT
> discount. If Kathleen had made a net capital gain on her collectables (her
> jewellery), she would also include it here.
>
> Next, Kathleen notes her capital loss from collectables … Although she made a
> capital loss from collectables, she can't reduce her other capital gains by
> this amount. However, she can carry this amount over so that if she makes a
> capital gain from collectables in the future, she can deduct this capital loss
> from her capital gain on a later tax return.

## Example 2: capital loss on the sale of shares (Kathleen)

> Using the facts from example 1, we will also assume that Kathleen has the
> following to consider:
>
> **Capital loss on the sale of 600 shares for $3 each on 25 June 2026**
>
> Kathleen had bought these shares on 10 October 2025 and each has a reduced
> cost base of $4 (including incidental costs of acquisition and disposal).
> Reduced cost base 600 × $4 = $2,400
> Capital proceeds 600 × $3 = $1,800
> Capital loss
> Reduced cost base − capital proceeds = capital loss
> $2,400 − $1,800 = $600
>
> Kathleen now has a $600 loss she can use to deduct from her capital gains.
> From the earlier example, we know Kathleen has a $3,000 capital gain
> calculated using the discount method. She has another capital gain of $520
> that she calculated using the 'other' method. Kathleen chooses to deduct the
> first $520 of her capital loss from the capital gain calculated using the
> 'other' method and to deduct the remaining $80 from the capital gain
> calculated using the discount method. Working this way gives her the best
> result:
>
> - 'other' method capital gain – $520
>   subtract capital loss of $520
>   equals $0 (zero)
> - discount method capital gain of $3,000
>   subtract capital loss of $80 ($600 − $520)
>   totals $2,920.
>
> Kathleen makes a note that she has capital gains of $2,920 calculated using
> the discount method.

## Example 3: unapplied net capital losses from earlier years (Kathleen)

> Kathleen has unapplied net capital losses from earlier years of $400 that
> aren't from collectables or personal use assets.
>
> In our example so far, Kathleen applied her current year capital loss and had
> $2,920 of capital gains calculated using the discount method remaining.
> Taking this example further, Kathleen would now also deduct the unapplied net
> capital losses of $400 from earlier income years from her capital gain of
> $2,920 calculated using the discount method:
>
> $2,920 − $400 = $2,520
>
> This leaves $2,520 of capital gains calculated using the discount method.
> Kathleen must use all current year capital losses and all the unapplied net
> capital losses from earlier years **before applying the CGT discount of 50%**.
> In this example, the amount at label V is still $500 because this is what she
> will carry forward as losses from collectables to future income years.

## Example 4: total capital gains calculated using the discount method (Kathleen)

> From our earlier information, we know Kathleen had capital gains of $2,520
> calculated using the discount method after applying relevant capital losses.
> She works out her total capital gains by multiplying her capital gain by the
> CGT discount of 50%:
>
> $2,520 × 50% = $1,260

## Example 5: question 18 – label A Net capital gain (Kathleen)

> Because no other CGT concessions apply to Kathleen, she writes **$1,260** at
> question 18 – label A Net capital gain in her supplementary tax return.

## Example 6: question 18 – label V (Kathleen)

> Kathleen has deducted all her current year capital losses (except those from
> collectables) and her net capital losses from earlier years from her capital
> gains in the order that gave her the best result. This means she will only
> have capital losses from collectables to carry forward to a later income year.
> Kathleen writes **$500** at question 18 – label V in her supplementary tax
> return.

## How this maps to this project

The Kathleen chain is *the* end-to-end net-capital-gain method, and the
`reports::net_capital_gain` report implements it: gains classified as
discount-eligible or not by the parcel's holding period, current-year losses
applied to non-discountable gains first, earlier-year losses next, the 50%
discount applied last to what remains, and any excess losses carried forward.
The share legs of Examples 1–5 are reproduced in `src/ato_examples.rs`
(`tax_return_18_kathleen_*`), asserting the ATO's $3,520 at 18H, the $520/$80
loss split, the $400 earlier-year deduction and the **$1,260** net capital gain
at 18A.

**Not modelled — the jewellery leg.** Collectables are a separate CGT asset
class whose losses are *quarantined*: they can only ever reduce capital gains
from collectables, so Kathleen's $500 jewellery loss never touches her share
gains and is carried forward separately (her label V figure). This project has
no collectables asset class and no quarantined-loss pool — every capital loss
enters one pool — so a collectable would be mis-modelled as an ordinary listing
whose loss wrongly offsets share gains. Entering collectables is out of scope
(a Known limitation in `docs/API.md`); the reproduced tests carry only
Kathleen's share legs, which is why the asserted label V is nil rather than the
ATO's $500.
